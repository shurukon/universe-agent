# Universe Agent — Master Prompt for a Realistic Memory Universe Sphere

Copy the prompt below in full. It is an art-direction and real-time 3D production specification for an AI generator or a 3D team. It is **not** a request for a generic space wallpaper or a conventional app interface.

```text
Design an ultra-premium desktop interface for **Universe Agent** in Voice Mode, where the complete interface is a **real, fully three-dimensional galactic memory sphere**. Do not make a dashboard. Do not make a flat space background. Do not make an empty glass orb. Do not fill a sphere with random colored dots. The required result is one large, convincing cosmic entity: a dense, living memory field that occupies roughly 76–88% of the desktop viewport height and appears suspended in an ink-black void. It must immediately read as the visible mind, spatial memory, and operational system of Universe Agent.

## Camera, Framing, and Composition

Use a calm three-quarter camera view, slightly above the sphere’s equatorial plane, with a medium perspective lens. The sphere must never look small, distant, distorted by an ultra-wide lens, or like a perfectly clean glass marble. Its boundary should be almost fully visible, with a very fine edge that fades naturally into darkness. A few weak background stars and faint cosmic dust may extend behind it to establish deep space, but all visual importance must remain inside the memory sphere.

Place a large **Cosmic Core** at the true center of the sphere. It must not be a white ball, a generic glowing orb, or a decorative black hole. Make it a dense, layered cognitive engine: a dark indigo inner mass with restrained cyan plasma, microscopic knowledge particles, translucent crystalline or network-like shells, and multiple thin orbital paths crossing at different inclinations. It should feel as if it is compressing, retrieving, linking, and synthesizing memory. Use thin graduated halos around it, never a blown-out white bloom that destroys detail.

Outside the sphere, use an extremely deep ink-black and near-navy background, with only sparse distant stars, very dim dust, and a natural dark vignette. Do not use huge cinematic nebula clouds, broad purple gradients, or bright wallpaper effects behind the globe.

## The Physical Cosmic Structure Inside the Sphere

Build the sphere as a coherent, volumetric astronomical object with several interlocked layers. It must have real depth, density gradients, foreground elements, middle-distance elements, far-distance elements, and visible occlusion. It must never read as a 2D spiral painted on a circular background.

1. **L5 long-term memory shell.** Form a faint, irregular dark-matter lattice around the outer volume. It must not be a uniform grid. Use delicate filament networks, sparse branching gravitational threads, distant star clusters, and weak indigo signals that become visible only at certain angles or during a relationship query. This layer represents enduring preferences, rules, remote relationships, long-running skills, and deep memory.

2. **Cognitive spiral arms.** Create four to six non-identical, volumetric spiral arms emerging from the Cosmic Core and flowing into the outer field. Each arm is made of millions of tiny sharp light particles, knowledge dust, and slow data currents. Give the arms thickness, turbulence, height variation, and depth; some must pass in front of the core while others disappear behind it. Use denser clusters, quiet gaps, faint nebular pockets, and believable gravitational asymmetry. Do not draw flat neon spirals or perfectly repeated arms.

3. **Skill and domain solar systems.** Distribute approximately 42–80 compact solar systems through the sphere, each representing a skill cluster or capability domain. Systems must vary meaningfully in scale, density, depth, and visual complexity, but must never look randomly scattered. Each system contains a small luminous central star, one to four thin elliptical orbital tracks at different inclinations, one to four small planets, occasional moons or asteroid belts, and a rare thin knowledge gate. Place systems throughout the sphere’s volume, not only on the surface and not only around the core.

4. **L3 crystallized-memory stars.** Add hundreds of small stars that represent facts, preferences, learned insights, decisions, and stable retrieved knowledge. Some are isolated; others appear as meaningful constellations and topic clusters. Use cool white for verified or resolved knowledge, Photon Cyan for active signal and live retrieval, and restrained violet for complex contextual memory. Stars must be sharp, minute, and varied in brightness. Avoid large blurry dots or identical sparkling particles.

5. **L1 experiential planets and work loops.** Inside the systems, create small believable planets with distinct materials: dark rocky planets with subtle topography, oceanic worlds with low cyan rim-light, cool icy worlds, rare metallic worlds with fine reflective seams, and occasional restrained gas giants with thin rings. They must look credible at closer zoom: terminator shadows, low atmospheric scattering, controlled star reflections, and material differences. Do not use cartoon planets, rainbow planets, toy planets, or oversized decorative spheres.

6. **L2 fragments, moons, and contextual debris.** Populate selected systems with fine moons, microscopic asteroid belts, dust fragments, and broken relationship trails. They represent snippets, files, notes, partial task results, sub-contexts, and memories in formation. Make the distribution asymmetric and physically plausible; do not place uniform rocks on every orbit.

7. **L4 compound memory nebulae.** Add 8–14 thin, irregular, volumetric nebulae inside the sphere. These represent compound skills, dense memories, and multi-agent context. They should breathe slowly between deep cyan and muted violet, remain transparent enough to reveal stars and orbits behind them, and never become opaque fog, white clouds, or flat 2D smoke.

8. **Knowledge gates and dark cores.** Add rare slim cyan or violet ring structures, tilted in space, to represent skill invocation or rapid context transfer. Add only three to five small cognitive dark cores, each with a precise indigo or faint amber accretion disk. They must be visually subordinate; no gigantic black hole may dominate the composition.

9. **Agent and task trajectories.** Include a limited number of tiny comet-like signal particles that move between systems along short, curved trajectories. Their transparent trails should be extremely fine. They represent agents carrying user intent, plans, retrieved context, work in progress, or results. They must not look like spaceships, laser beams, game particles, or straight lines cutting through the globe.

## Universe Agent Memory Semantics

This is not a decorative astronomy catalog. Every visual category belongs to Universe Agent’s memory and capability model. The Cosmic Core integrates user intent, planning, retrieval, and task execution. Solar systems are skill and domain clusters, such as development, design, research, writing, analysis, automation, and scheduled work, but the default view must display no text labels. Stars are crystallized memories. Planets are work loops, active tasks, and experiential records. Moons and asteroids are snippets, files, notes, and sub-context fragments. Nebulae are compound skills or complex memory clusters. The dark-matter lattice is long-term memory, remote association, preferences, and rules. Gates are skill calls or rapid context transitions. Moving agent particles are quiet operational activity.

The organization must feel organic and astronomical, never like a flat technical schematic. The default scene must contain no labels, sidebars, dashboard cards, UI grids, windows, visible text, or explanatory badges. A person should understand that this is a memory universe through depth, hierarchy, behavior, and relationships—not because the scene has labels pasted on top of it.

## Distinct Light and Motion Behavior for Every Element

Treat light as a system language, not a random special effect. Each class must respond differently and consistently:

**Cosmic Core.** In idle mode, it breathes slowly from deep cyan within indigo. In listening mode, inner particles pull slightly toward the center and fine cyan halos grow clearer. In reasoning mode, internal orbital currents rotate and a small number of light paths travel into relevant spiral arms. In execution mode, short controlled signal flows travel from the core to selected systems. In response mode, a soft cool-white confirmation travels outward and fades. Never use a giant explosion, full-screen flash, or huge shockwave rings.

**Memory stars.** Each star has an asynchronous micro-twinkle, from barely visible shimmer to a restrained flare according to its importance. On hover or selection, only that star’s corona increases, and a few short relationship traces may appear toward relevant nearby elements.

**Solar systems.** When a system is focused, its transparent volumetric boundary becomes slightly more visible, its orbits illuminate sequentially, and its planets increase orbital activity by a subtle amount while the rest of the sphere remains calm.

**Planets.** On focus, reveal a sharper atmospheric rim, a refined surface highlight, and perhaps a tiny pulse travelling along its own orbit. Do not turn the planet into a fully neon sphere.

**Nebulae.** On activation, expand only very slowly by a small amount, increase the visibility of their internal particles, and return gradually—as if a memory is being retrieved from deep context.

**Knowledge gates.** Rotate delicately, increase brightness only slightly, and allow a small number of particles to cross through. They are quiet knowledge-transfer structures, not loud video-game portals.

**Dark cores.** Use inverse reaction: the interior darkens briefly, nearby stars dim subtly, and the thin accretion ring becomes more precise. Never turn this into a dramatic black-hole effect.

**L5 lattice.** When a long-term relationship is relevant, one or a few remote lattice paths brighten gradually through the depth of the sphere and then fade away.

**Agent particles.** Move along restrained curved paths with different faint speeds and subtle semantic colors. Do not fill the entire field with constant motion.

Most of the mass must be nearly still, so that an interaction has weight and meaning. Use micro-motion only: slow core rotation, planetary orbits, asynchronous star twinkles, gentle dust currents, sparse agent trajectories, and slow nebular breathing. Do not use fast sphere rotation, racing stars, aggressive parallax, predictable looping, or continuous spectacle.

## Lighting, Materials, Rendering, and Color Discipline

Use premium physically inspired real-time rendering: ACES-style filmic tone mapping, exceptionally soft shadows, light volumetric scattering, subtle internal depth haze, controlled additive bloom, fine ambient occlusion around close elements, and excellent anti-aliasing. Tiny details must remain sharp rather than becoming fog or oversized glow blobs. The scene must read clearly on a dark desktop display without clipping highlights or crushing all dark values.

The palette must be disciplined. Use ink-black and deep navy for space. Use **Photon Cyan #38C8FF** for active signal, listening, and retrieval. Use quiet indigo and violet for memory and compound context. Use cool white for verified knowledge and response. Use a very rare low-intensity amber only for temporal or special gravitational states. Do not use dense reds, pinks, greens, yellows, rainbow systems, or a different saturated color for every object. Color must communicate function, never decorate arbitrarily.

Every object requires a plausible material. Planets need differentiated roughness, metalness, topology, and atmosphere. Rings need small breaks and irregular illumination. Dust needs varied particle sizes and density. Stars need a compact bloom. Nebulae need layered transparency. Memory lattices must remain very thin and should only become obvious at closer inspection or activation. Avoid generic repeated spheres, large blurry sprites, flat icons, perfectly clean geometric lines, identical orbit systems, and repeated decorative circles.

## Interaction, Camera, Accessibility, and Performance

The sphere must be gently draggable around its axis, smoothly zoomable within safe limits, and selectable by mouse, touch, or keyboard focus. Prevent the camera from entering the core or losing the sphere completely. On hover, communicate through local light and contrast before showing any textual tooltip. On selection, ease the camera slightly toward the chosen element while preserving the sensation of a complete enclosing memory sphere. Only later, when a product layer is intentionally added, may a separate contextual information layer appear.

In reduced-motion mode, stop nonessential drifting and periodic glow while retaining static contrast and short, purposeful selection feedback. Make desktop performance reliable using GPU point fields, instancing, level-of-detail for distant systems, and lower particle density on small or weak devices.

## Final Quality Bar

The result must not be a generic space illustration, game environment, screensaver, cyberpunk wallpaper, or data visualization. It must be the visible mind of Universe Agent: a large, deep, dense, organized, explorable, astronomically convincing memory universe that can naturally appear to grow as Universe learns and works, without becoming empty, random, noisy, or overfilled.

Quality target: cinematic AAA real-time 3D, premium desktop spatial computing, high-density astrophysical detail, physically inspired materials, precise depth, restrained glow, sharp micro-stars, volumetric memory nebulae, coherent orbital mechanics, no visible UI in the default scene, no text labels, no sidebars, no dashboard cards, and no generic sci-fi wallpaper.
```
